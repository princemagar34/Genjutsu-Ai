Privacy Policy

Last updated 19 November 2026

Genjutsu is an email security tool. To protect your inbox it has to read your email, so this page explains exactly what it reads, what it keeps, who it shares it with, and how to have it deleted. In short: we use your data only to scan your mail and run the features you turn on, we never sell it, and we never use it for advertising or to train AI models.

1. Who we are

Genjutsu is developed and operated by Prince Paija Magar in Kathmandu, Nepal ("we", "us"). For any privacy question or request, email princemagar338@gmail.com.

Genjutsu does not use advertising, analytics or tracking cookies.

3. How we use it

  • Scan your incoming email and label it Safe, Worth a look or Dangerous.
  • Move an email to spam or junk, or create a filter, only when you block it, turn on "Quarantine dangerous emails", or create a quarantine rule. You can restore any of them from the Blocked tab.
  • Send a weekly security digest to your own inbox, only if you turn it on.
  • Forward a phishing email to the Anti-Phishing Working Group only when you press "Report phishing".
  • Send replies from your account only in honeypot mode, and only to scam emails you choose to engage. Genjutsu never replies to no-reply addresses.
  • Write an AI summary of an email only when you press "Generate AI Summary".
  • Show your dashboard, history and reports, and send the alerts you have switched on.
  • Keep the service secure, investigate abuse, and fix problems.

We do not sell your data, use it for advertising, or use it to train machine-learning or AI models.

4. Who we share it with

We share data only with the service providers below, and only what each needs to do its job for you:

  • Google (Gmail API) and Microsoft (Microsoft Graph): to read, label and send mail in your mailbox, depending on which one you sign in with.
  • Your email provider's mail servers (for example iCloud or Yahoo): to read and send mail when you sign in with an app password.
  • Google Safe Browsing: links found in your emails are checked against Google's list of unsafe sites.
  • Domain registration lookups (RDAP): the domain names of senders and links (not the email itself) are looked up in public registration records to see how new they are.
  • Anti-Phishing Working Group (APWG): only when you press "Report phishing", the original email is forwarded from your account to reportphishing@apwg.org.
  • Discord: only if you connect a webhook, the subject, sender, score and main reason of flagged emails are sent to your Discord channel.
  • AI providers (Groq, and Anthropic if enabled): when you request an AI summary or use honeypot mode, the relevant email text is sent to generate the summary or reply.
  • Hosting providers: our database is hosted by Neon, and the app runs on cloud hosting (Google Cloud and Cloudflare).
  • Telegram: if the operator has enabled Telegram alerts, the subject, sender and threat score of flagged emails may be sent as an alert to the operator's Telegram chat.

We may also disclose information if the law requires it, or to protect the rights and safety of users and the service.

5. Google user data

Genjutsu's use and transfer to any other app of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. In particular, Gmail data is used only to provide and improve the user-facing features described above, is not used for advertising, is not sold, is not used to train AI or machine-learning models, and is not read by people except with your permission, for security purposes, to comply with the law, or where it has been made anonymous.

6. Who can see your data

Genjutsu administrators can see scan records (sender, subject, threat level and reasons), your account settings, rules and trusted senders, and whether your mailbox connection is working, so they can review false alarms, block scam domains for everyone and help when scanning breaks. They can also see details scammers sent in honeypot conversations, such as bank accounts or wallets, so these can be reported. They cannot see your password or your email tokens. Administrators sign in with a password and a code from their phone, and every administrator action is recorded in an activity log.

7. How long we keep it

  • Account details, scan records, honeypot conversations and settings are kept while your account exists.
  • Sign-in sessions expire after 7 days.
  • Demo mode uses made-up sample emails. Nothing is sent, no mailbox is connected, and the demo account and its records are deleted within a day.
  • When you ask us to delete your account, we delete these records within 30 days, unless the law requires us to keep something longer.

8. Your choices and rights

  • Access or correct your data, or get a copy of it: email us.
  • Delete your account and data: use Settings → Delete account, which deletes everything immediately and revokes Google access, or email princemagar338@gmail.com from the address you signed in with.
  • Withdraw access at any time: remove Genjutsu from your Google account permissions or your Microsoft account permissions, or revoke the app password you created.
  • Turn off honeypot mode, quarantine, the weekly digest and alerts in Settings, and remove trusted senders, rules or your Discord connection at any time.
  • Export your threats and account activity at any time from Settings → Export to a SIEM. Files you download are yours to handle; we are not responsible for where you send them.
  • Revoke any API key in Settings; it stops working immediately.

9. Security

Connections are encrypted with HTTPS, sign-in tokens, app passwords and webhook addresses are encrypted at rest, requests are rate-limited, and access to the database is restricted. No system is completely secure, so if we ever discover a breach that affects your data we will tell you without undue delay.

10. Children

Genjutsu is not intended for children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect their data. Organizations using Genjutsu are responsible for using it only with staff or student accounts they are allowed to protect.

11. Changes

If we change this policy we will update the date above, and for significant changes we will tell you in the app before they take effect.
